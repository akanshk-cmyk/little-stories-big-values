FROM A SOURCE TO A STORY

What happens after I add a link or PDF?

Your reviewed source can now be used by the AI story writer when you select it in Story Studio. Uploading alone does not select or approve it. Read the preview, give permission for this exact text, then select the source when creating a story.

Add a source or open a saved source to follow its progress.

  1. Available

    1. Add your source

    A web link asks the website for its public page. An uploaded PDF goes to your local app. Neither action asks the AI to write a story.

  2. Available

    2. Read the words

    The app extracts readable text and shows its page or paragraph locations. Pictures in scanned PDFs are not read. Web pages behind a login or dependent on scripts may not work.

  3. Available

    3. Save and inspect

    The extracted words are saved on your computer in the app's source shelf. Check the preview for missing or mixed-up text. It stays Pending review until you explicitly allow it for story use. Parent review is not expert cultural certification.

  4. Available

    4. Select reviewed sources

    Allow a source after reading its preview. In Story Studio, select up to three sources and describe your idea. The app finds up to five matching passages and sends them to the writer. If none matches, it asks you to change the idea or source.

  5. Separate AI path

    5. Create an original story

    Story Studio sends your preferences and selected source passages to Nebius Qwen. The saved draft shows the exact passages and the story details linked to them. With no sources selected, original stories use your preferences; festivals use built-in notes.

  6. Checks required

    6. Review, approve and export

    The saved draft goes through QA. Once it passes, you review and approve that exact version. PDF export then becomes available; narration also needs ElevenLabs. Changes require another review.

Your next step

Read the preview and allow the source, then open Story Studio and select it. Only selected, reviewed source passages are sent to the writer. Traditional retellings remain unavailable; sacred-figure scenes stay imagined fiction.
